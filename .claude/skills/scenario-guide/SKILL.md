Make the beats worth staging before `direction.ts` locks them — this layer invents the content (premise, character, beat chain); `direction-guide` checks the structure that carries it, `staging-guide` makes a locked beat read on screen.

## Premise is the engine

- **A premise is a collision, not a topic** — a specific trait × a situation that punishes it ("a meticulous butler serves on a pirate ship": etiquette × mayhem), the classic **fish-out-of-water** shape. A topic ("life aboard a pirate ship") generates description; a collision generates beats.
- **Test: list five beats in one breath** — a working premise makes beats fall out, each a new way the collision fires; if inventing beats feels like pulling, fix the premise, never pad the beats.
- **State both sides of the collision in the logline** — so every later judgment can check against it.

## Character is where beats come from

- **Give each character a want and a trait that fights it** — the screenwriter's **want-vs-flaw** pair; behavior falls out of it (the butler wants a flawless dinner service; the sea doesn't care). A character described only by look generates poses, not beats.
- **Specific enough that two writers stage the same beat** — "clumsy" allows anything; "cannot let a crooked fork lie" writes the shot by itself.
- **The payoff resolves through who the character is, not despite it** — the flaw becomes the skill (the obsessively polished tray is what blinds the boarding party).
- **Never fire the same expression unchanged** — a repeat must escalate (scale, cost, witness, consequence) or be a planted callback.

## Pitch wide, cull hard

- **Write the obvious version first, then ban it** — the pitch anyone would make from the brief is the one the viewer has already seen; write it in one line so you recognize it inside every later pitch, and never ship it unchanged.
- **Invent eight to ten before choosing three** — each a different collision, or the same trait fired in a different arena, at a different cost, before a different witness. Three invented cold are the obvious one and two variations of it.
- **Cull by six questions, one line each** — the **hook**: what the first two seconds put on screen that stops a thumb (a pitch opening on calm has none); the **collision**: both sides stated in the logline; the **nearest thing**: the closest piece a viewer has seen, and the difference in the idea (no stateable difference is the obvious version); the **final image**: the last frame you can picture, and whether it is the feeling asked for as a picture; the **share line**: what a viewer says handing it on ("it's cute" is a pleasure, not a line); the **model's reach**: whether the payoff rests on what AI video drops — a hand-off, legible text, a crowd (`production-guide`).
- **Whatever the hearing banned is a cut wherever it shows** (`drafting-guide` step 2) — a feeling other than the one they asked for, a surface it cannot play on (a feed with the sound off needs the picture to carry it); however good the pitch.
- **Keep one safe and one strange among the three** — the safe one is the yardstick; three near each other let the human choose nothing.

## Chain beats with therefore/but, never and-then

- **Each beat's consequence is the next beat's situation** — the South Park writers' **"but/therefore" rule**: "therefore" (the soup spills, therefore the deck turns slippery) or "but" (the table is finally set, but the cannons fire); beats joined by "and then" can be reordered without breaking, which means no story.
- **Test: delete a beat** — if nothing downstream breaks, it's decoration or the chain is fake; rewire or cut.
- **The chain links beats, not every shot** — the rules within a beat (one action, the within-beat change, continuity vs montage) are `direction-guide`'s.

## Name the prop, not the quality

- **An `action` names concrete objects and motions, never qualities** — "chaos on deck" stages nothing; "plates sliding, a tureen rolling for the rail, the tablecloth dragged overboard" is the beat. The specific prop is the joke or the emotion's carrier.
- **Plant a prop early and re-fire it late** — **Chekhov's gun**, comedy's **setup → callback**: the cheapest structure a short piece can afford. Plant the object that actually fires, not "something ominous in the corner", and keep it peripheral — a plant that steals its beat's frame is cramming, not setup.

## Sharpen by lens

- **comedy** — the violation breaks a pattern the piece established (no pattern, no laugh); escalation raises stakes, not volume — each gag **tops** the last (the topper); the button recontextualizes — the last beat reframes what came before (the butler irons the Jolly Roger: the ship has a butler now).
- **mini-drama** — pressure is visible **stakes**, a **ticking clock** when possible (water rising past the second step, never "tension mounts"); the hero beat spends exactly what the pressure banked — the bigger the debt, the better the payoff.
- **Other lenses** — read the lens's beat roles (`direction-guide`'s lenses.md) as craft obligations to fulfill, never slots to fill.

## Pitch test

- **Tell the shot list aloud in one breath** — a beat that needs explaining won't read on screen; whatever you'd naturally skip, cut.
- **The takeaway is the last image** — Save the Cat's **final image**: say what the viewer carries away (the `brief`'s audience field); the final beat must be that sentence as a picture.

## At length

- **A piece with acts runs every test here per act, never per piece** — five beats, one collision, the delete test, the one-breath pitch.
- **One collision per act, one question per piece** — a B-story is a second want-vs-flaw pair in its own arena; the brief's logline unifies the acts, and the final image answers it.

## Pitfalls

- **Role restatement as action** — "escalation: things get worse" fills the slot and says nothing.
- **Inventing beats before the premise** — beats that don't share one collision read as a compilation, not a piece.
- **A premise that fires one way** — if every beat is the same collision in the same arena, it's a sketch, not a story; vary what it costs, who sees it, what breaks.
