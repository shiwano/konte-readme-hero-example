**Author** the video's direction in `direction.ts` before any stage shot is written. Done = `konte status` reports no unresolved direction finding; not the generated media's review.

## Authoring the direction

Write every prose field (brief, `action`, roster `name`/`description`) in the human's language — the conversation's. `promptDepiction` is the one exception: it is the model's language.

### The brief

- **Keep it short — only what stays true across the piece.** Pacing goes in as _intent_ (cut rhythm, where it builds), not a per-shot second count.
- **`hook` is the first two seconds as one sentence** — what the opening frame puts on screen that stops a scroll: the collision, the prop that will fire, a face wanting something. The first beat's `action` realizes it, and `polish-guide` windows the first shot on it.
- **`look` names the medium first** — live action, anime, 3DCG, stop motion — then palette, framing, cut rhythm.
- **`tolerances` — a flaw the whole piece will carry** (a brand mark that wanders into frame, unreadable fine text), agreed like any other field. "This take is fine, move on" is an accept.

### Shape

- **The whole file, every field once** — [skeleton.md](references/skeleton.md).
- **One `sequence` node — `lens`, `pleasure`, `shots[]`** — the beats in order. The stages mint shots from these ids (`authoring-guide`); prose "why / what / tone" lives in `brief`.
- **A beat that is not story is `kind: "aside"`** — a title card, an eyecatch, an OP or ED dropped in whole. It holds a span and a `label`, and nothing the arc reads — no `role`/`setup`/`action`/`script` (a beat needing a spoken line is a narrative beat). Arc and pacing checks skip it, so an OP never dilutes the act ratios; coverage does not, so an unrealized one still blocks export. The animatic never boards one — `layout-guide`.
- **A beat with no camera is `kind: "graphic"`** — a UI screen, a chart, a motion graphic: an arc beat (`role`/`action`/`script`/`telop` all read) with no `setup`/`lineup`/`lineupTo`/`join`. A character in frame → narrative; a screen or graphic → graphic; not story → aside. A character inside a graphic cannot be declared — make it a narrative beat, or wipe them in with `cutin`.
- **`setup` is the frame the beat is taken from, required per narrative shot** — a `setups` roster id. Its size and its place are read through it, so a shot never declares `framing` or `location` of its own and two beats on one setup cannot disagree about either.
- **`policy` is required and acceptance-hashed** (`lenses` is the one optional top-level field). Keep `fps` a multiple of 8, 24 in practice, so a whole-second shot lands on the models' frame step. The working canvas is **derived**, never written — `size.delivery` is the frame that ships and the only declared aspect (`1920×1080`, `1080×1920`), `size.megapixels` the generation budget konte resolves against it, landing the nearest 32-grid canvas (`0.9` → `1248×704`); generation time scales with pixel count, so ~0.9 is the default, turned down when iteration drags, and a delivery well above the canvas needs the video's `export.delivery.upscale`. `lang` + `fonts` are the typesetting every stage reads: `lang` is the piece's language (`"ja"`, `"en"` — a closed list, one per piece; a script or region may ride along, `"zh-Hant"`), deciding line-breaking and the glyphs no font covers (`composition-guide` for a line in another script); `fonts` are Google Fonts family names in fallback order — declaring none leaves text on whatever face the machine has, and a script no default face covers (CJK, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Thai) blocks the spend with `fonts-undeclared`. `speech` is `"none"｜"no-dialogue"｜"free"`.
- **`script` is spoken; `telop` is not** — `script` (a character line, a mob voice, or narration) is the dialogue source of truth, injected into both stages so subtitles derive from here, and every line demands a voice plus an `<Audio>` on that beat's animatic shot carrying **that line's words** (`composition-guide`). A `ja` line's notation decides its reading — [japanese-notation.md](references/japanese-notation.md). A **character** line also takes `acting`: **one line** on how it is said ("wheedling, drawn out, a shade too loud") — never a list of acoustic properties, which comes back stiff. A mob line and narration take none. `telop` is unspoken on-screen text — a title, a lower third, a speaker-less caption — and demands neither. Words inside the world (a sign) are the image prompt's, not telop.

### Rosters — `characters`, `props`, `locations`

- **All three are anchored rosters** — a record keyed by `id` (one shared namespace), each `id` matching an exposed `reference:<id>` asset: generate the subject once there and animate/compose from it — drawing one straight into a panel or a keyframeless shot is what the direction check catches.
- **`characters` (required) and `props` (optional) are referenced by exact `name` in every `action`** — a distinctive phrase ("the black cat"), since konte matches a literal substring. Only a character may speak, and only characters carry the reference-acceptance gate; a prop resolves to its newest ready variant.
- **A character also declares `promptDepiction`, what a prompt calls them by** — a noun **the model can draw**, in the model's language: `chef` yes, `owner` no — a role names its place in the story and carries no look, and `Konte-chan` names a person the model has never seen. The story's words for them are `name`, `description` and `action`. Carry the least that tells this one from the others in the piece — `tabby` where there is one cat, `girl in a purple hoodie` where a bare `girl` is two people. No adapter notation: the notation wraps around the noun. No declared depiction may contain another (`prompt-depiction-conflict`), characters and landmarks in one space — konte's scan is a substring. konte scans each keyframe's prompt for it (`subject-unnamed`) and never writes it into one.
- **`locations` (required)** — the places, each anchored to a `reference:<id>`. A shot reaches its location through its setup, so no shot names one directly; the sheet is the place's **master** — seen from its main camera position, each setup on that axis a window cut from it (`staging-guide`).
- **A location declares `landmarks`, what only this place has** — required and non-empty, keyed by id, each `{ name, promptDepiction, description }` — its `promptDepiction` under the same rule as a character's, in the same one depiction space. A frame with nothing particular to the set in it comes back as another room, and no sheet fixes that — the thing in frame does. A landmark anchors on no `reference:<id>`; the plate and its `animatic:landmark.<id>` crop are its anchors. Its id shares the direction's one id space, so it may not collide with a character, a prop, a place, or a landmark elsewhere.

### The camera — `setups`

- **`setups` (required) is the coverage plan** — a record keyed by `id`: one camera position, the place it stands in, and the size it takes. Every shot names one. Read down the roster it is the whole piece's coverage; read down the shot list it is the size and space cadence the pacing rules below are about.
- **`framing` is the shot size, declared on the setup** — of the `action`'s mover: `wide` (geography), `medium` (the readable default), `close` (face/detail, the reaction), `insert` (an object fills frame — plant and payoff). One setup is one size; the same position at two sizes is two setups.
- **It is the ONLY roster with no `reference:<id>`** — a setup binds a frame, not an identity. What realizes it is a **plate**: an `animatic:plate.<setupId>` asset holding that frame with nobody in it, declared under `plates` in animatic.tsx (`staging-guide`).
- **A setup two or more shots share owes a plate, and so do the `wide` and the `medium` of one `within` axis when the piece cuts between them** — `setup-unrealized` and `axis-unrealized` draw those two lines; nothing else owes one.
- **Frames must differ, and a place lived in needs one it returns to** — two setups with the same `location`/`framing`/`holds` order is `setup-indistinct` (inserts exempt); a location with 8+ shots, fewer than half on a shared frame, is `setup-atomized`.
- **Name a setup for what it frames, not for the beat that first used it** — `samuraiClose`, not `shot06`. Beats move; the frame is what other beats come back to.
- **`holds` is what the frame carries of its place, left to right** — landmark ids of the setup's own location, required and non-empty on every framing but `insert` (which fills the frame with an object and shows no set). It is the frame's property, not a beat's: the camera is fixed, so every beat on the setup carries the same things. konte prints it as the `set:` line of `konte inspect --prompts`, demands a plate's sentence name each one (`plate-unnamed`), and flags two frames of one place that disagree about which side something is on (`landmark-flipped`, keyed by whichever id sorts later — an intended reverse angle is the waiver).
- **`within` is the wider frame this one is a window of** — a setup of the same place with a strictly wider `framing` (steps may be skipped), or `null` where the frame is the root of its own camera axis; an `insert` is off the axis and declares neither. konte demands the declaration wherever a wider frame of the place holds what this one holds (`within-undeclared`, structural) and never derives it — that shape is as often a reverse or a second axis. What it buys is the plates: a window's plate is cut inside its parent's (`plate-unnested`, `staging-guide`), so a `close` comes back closer than its `medium` rather than at whatever distance its own plate was drawn at.

### Who is on screen — `lineup`

- **`lineup` is who THIS frame holds, left to right** — `characters` ids, required on every beat. Someone with only a sleeve in frame is in the list; someone the frame does not hold is absent — there is no off-screen axis. A frame holding no one (a prop insert, an empty place) declares `lineup: []`.
- **A prop is never lined up** — the row has one axis and a prop sits in front of or behind as often as beside. Write where it is relative to whoever holds it or set it down, in the prompt.
- **Write the frame, not the roster** — a push-in onto one person lists that person. konte remembers the continuity itself, accumulating "a is left of b" per location, and a later beat that contradicts an earlier one is `lineup-flipped`.
- **`lineupTo` is the frame the shot LEAVES, in full** — declare it wherever the frame changes inside the shot: a move, an entrance, an exit, or a change meant to land on the next cut. Symmetrical with `lineup`, so list everyone the closing frame holds; whoever you leave out has walked out of it, and their place stops being known until a later frame holds them again. The beats after it read against it. Identical to the `lineup` is `lineup-vacuous` — leave it out.
- **A camera coming round reverses everyone, so list everyone** — half a frame re-declared reads as the rest walking out of shot, and nothing will catch it.
- **A frame holds whoever it spans** — two people with a third between them cannot be framed together without that third in shot, sleeve or shoulder (`lineup-gap`). Either list them, or the beat is on a frame that does not hold both ends.
- **`join` is what the boundary INTO this beat is** — leave it out for an ordinary cut with story time running on; `"jump-back"` enters a flashback and `"jump-forward"` skips ahead or leaves one, and both empty the accumulated order everywhere (given time, people move). `"continuous"` says there is no cut at all: this beat and the one before it are one unbroken take.
- **Where the beat before is on the same `setup`, `join` is required** — a boundary with time running on and the camera not moving has nothing to cut on, so say which it is: `"continuous"` for a long take, `"jump-forward"` for a jump cut or a gap, `"jump-back"` for a flashback. Nowhere else is a choice demanded, and `"continuous"` is refused there — a different setup, an aside or graphic beat in between, or the opening beat cannot be one take. A `jump-back`/`jump-forward` is written wherever story time breaks, on any boundary.
- **A long take is one camera position** — a hold whose camera moves is one beat with two `<Panel>`s. Two `"continuous"` beats must also agree on the frame between them: the first one's `lineupTo` (or its `lineup`) is the second one's `lineup`, and the video has to pin that one frame.
- **`cutin` is a second camera frame over the beat** — `{ setup, lineup, lineupTo?, join? }` on a narrative or graphic beat (the presenter wiped in over a screen). It is one more shot on that setup: every lineup, plate and keyframe rule applies, findings keyed `<shotId>.cutin`. `cutin.join` is the wipe's own boundary, on the same terms against the cutin over the beat before. Placement is the composition's; an inset no camera takes (a phone screen) needs no `cutin`.
- **The board answers to it** — every subject the frame holds must reach a keyframe as a `reference:<id>` input (`character-unconsumed`), in the declared order (`slot-order-mismatch`). All of these are waivable and all of them gate an animatic/video spend.

### Casting voices — `voice`, `narrator`

- **A script line demands a voice** — a `{ character }` line requires `voice` on that roster entry, a `{ narration }` line the top-level `narrator`. `{ speaker }` lines are unchecked.
- **`id` is its own `reference:<id>` sample**, never the look anchor; `description` is how they sound (sex, age, timbre, pace) — an empty one is a hard error. One voice heard twice (a narrator who is the protagonist, twins) names one `id` twice, each with its own `description`.
- **Nothing to waive for text on screen** — that is `telop`, which asks for no voice. `konte generate animatic` and `konte generate video` both abort with `VOICE_ACCEPTANCE_REQUIRED` until each sample is accepted.

### The arc

- **The lens fixes the beat order (its payoff beat is the climax); `pleasure` names the feeling it aims for** — pick each from the catalogs in [lenses.md](references/lenses.md); `pleasure` is a closed vocabulary, a custom `defineLens` a last resort (rules there too).
- **A building beat must actually repeat** — `rhythm` and `process` each need at least two consecutive, varied shots (a copy is not variation); `comedy`'s optional `escalation` escalates across shots rather than holding one.
- **Write the hinge beats as a change, and earn the climax** — on a **turn** (`disruption`/`violation`/`solution` — the **inciting incident** at this scale) and the **payoff**, the `action` must state a change of state, not a standing situation: name the two concrete, visible states it moves between ("the calm sill; the ball rolls in"). A payoff with nothing grounding/turning/building before it is unearned; a cold open waives it.

### Writing the shots

- **One action per beat — one sentence, one mover, one motion** — a second sentence, a second mover, or a then-pivot is the next shot's `action`. Test: **if it splits into two sentences that each still stage something, it's two shots**. A role is a phase, not a shot count — expect the shot list to outnumber the lens's beat roles.
- **A gag or turn is a shot chain, never one shot** — trigger → reaction (→ collateral): the thing that moves (the rolling tureen) gets its own shot before the subject responds, and the reaction shot is often the stronger one. Expand the chain as you draft, never as a later split of one row.
- **A key action is a chain too — never one take** — anticipation, the motion elided in the cut, the result landed; this and the other idioms are [cut-idioms.md](references/cut-idioms.md).
- **Keep one ledger of the world's state** — where each character is, what they hold, what broke: damage never resets. A state may change in the cut — the next shot's opening just has to make it read (an aftermath `insert`, a sound, the prop now in hand); only a continuous-space stretch owes each transit a shot of its own.
- **Constructive space is the default** — build the space in the viewer's head from fragments (`medium`/`close`/`insert` glued by eyelines, screen direction, and recurring anchors — the staging is `staging-guide`'s): geography never shown whole is never asked to match, the grammar generation holds best.
- **Continuity is the marked spend** — one connected space, cause and effect crossing frames, every transit on screen; choose it only when the physical path _is_ the content (a chase, the tureen rolling for the rail), since it binds backgrounds and direction across every cut it spans.
- **A montage** (building beats: `escalation`, `rhythm`, `pressure`) juxtaposes parallel instances of one idea and lets the Kuleshov effect supply the connection — three unconnected shots of havoc read as "chaos grows" (the rule of three); inventing a physical link there is contrived and the hardest thing to stage. It still varies and ascends toward the peak, and waives only the cause-and-effect link — the ledger holds through it.
- **The direction check covers structure, not the frame** — on-screen legibility is yours: a _visible_ `pressure` (a queue, a timer), a payoff you read at a glance, a beat's movement legible in its shot alone.

## Clearing direction findings

`konte generate` / `reroll` / `export` abort with `DIRECTION_CHECK_FAILED` while the scope has an unresolved finding. `konte status` shows each with its cause and a waiver key in `[brackets]`. Characters, prop, location and setup findings are deferred — surfacing and gating only once the direction is accepted.

- **Fix it** — adjust the definition the finding points at: add/reorder a beat, shorten an over-long hold, fill an empty action. A **characters**/**props**/**locations** finding is fixed in the reference stage, not the roster: no reference asset → expose `reference:<id>` in `reference.tsx`; an unused entry → reference it in a shot (`action`) or a setup (`location`), or drop it from the roster. A **staging** finding is fixed in the animatic where it names the board: `character-unconsumed` → feed the named shot's keyframe `reference.<characterId>`; `subject-unnamed` → call them by their `promptDepiction` in that keyframe's prompt; `plate-undescribed` → write `plates.<id>.prompt` into the named shots' own prompts; `plate-unnamed` → name the landmark in that plate's own sentence; `landmark-unrealized` → return the landmark's crop under `landmarks`; `landmark-unconsumed` → feed the named keyframe `landmarks.<id>`. `landmark-flipped` is the direction's: fix the `holds` that has the place's axis wrong, or waive the reverse angle. A **setups** finding is fixed in the animatic: `setup-unrealized` → declare the plate under `plates`, keyed by the roster id; `axis-unrealized` → plate both ends of the named cut; `plate-unanchored` → feed `reference.<location>` to the plate; `setup-unconsumed` → feed the named shots' keyframes their anchor (`plates.<id>`, or `reference.<location>` with no plate); `unused-setup` → point a shot at it or drop it. Two are the direction's own: `setup-indistinct` → merge the twins, or move the camera so the frames differ; `setup-atomized` → put a second shot on a frame the piece returns to.
- **Or waive it** — copy the `[bracketed]` key into the owning node's `waivers` with a real reason, only when the structure is a deliberate choice, never to silence a fixable finding; status flags stale waivers, and a key naming no finding code is a structural error (`waiver-unknown-code`) naming the key you meant.

## Pacing — rhythm, not uniform length

- **Every `duration` is a multiple of 0.5s, at least 0.5s** — the beat's span is the window the render cuts the take to. Off the grid is waivable. An adapter taking a `duration` input still wants a whole second — pass it one and let the window cut it.
- **A cut's pleasure lives in the _contrast_ between shot lengths** — equal-length cuts read as a metronome (three identical `duration`s in a row is the checker's line; the failure starts earlier): choose each length against its neighbors — staccato beats build pressure, a held frame releases; accelerate into a peak, ease after.
- **`wide` is a spend — establish once per act, when the space itself is content** — a second `wide` of one location inside one `role` is the checker's line; waive it when the space itself changed. A new act re-establishes by right: the line crosses neither a leaf boundary nor a role change, so a closing `wide` that bookends an opening one is unflagged.
- **Vary the framing size against its neighbors** — three same-size setups in a row is the checker's line: hold `medium`, push to `close`/`insert` before a payoff. A gag/turn's trigger is often an `insert`/`close`, its reaction a `close`.
- **A one-step size change inside one `location` invites background matching** — adjacent `wide`→`medium` (or `medium`→`close`) keeps most of the set in both frames, a match generation can't hold: jump two sizes (`wide`→`close`/`insert`), break the pair with an `insert` or a reaction shot, or keep it only when the match _is_ the cut (an axial punch-in, a match cut). The `wide`↔`medium` case is the checker's line; tighter pairs are yours to judge.
- **A held, low-change frame holds attention ~3s; earn a longer hold with change** — a budget, not a law: motion, an `<Animate>` move, or an overlay reveal buys seconds, and what dulls tempo is a frame held past its content. A comedy `button` cuts on the laugh, never past it.
- **A grounding or settling beat can't swallow the runtime** — lenses budget the `ground` beat at ≤40% and the `settle` beat at ≤20–25%; over is waivable (`atmosphere` is exempt).
- **A motif is one beat of feeling — each shot advances it, never restates it** (advancing = the therefore/but chain, `scenario-guide`): a motivated leap is fine, an arbitrary one is not, and a repeated beat (a long fight, a montage that keeps moving) is one motif stretched thin.

## Story structure

- **Front-load setups, resolve them later, never leave a gap to disengage** — don't open with your thesis; short scales to establish → develop → pay off.
- **The first beat is the hook** — its `action` puts `brief.hook` on screen in the first shot; the ground beat's job is who and where, never calm before anything happens.
- **A short piece is one leaf node (`sequence: { lens, shots }`); a long one gives it `sequences`** — acts, meta-lens, runtime balance → [long-form.md](references/long-form.md)
